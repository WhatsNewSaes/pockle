# Pixel League

A retro sports companion for the **Waveshare ESP32-S3-ePaper-3.97**. The first release supports MLB, NFL, NBA, and NCAA Division I FBS college football.

## Portrait display and team logos

The interface is 480 × 800 portrait with no outer frame. The home screen has five tabs — ALL, MLB, NFL, NBA, CFB — and a gear (Settings) at the end of the strip. On the tab row, up/down moves between tabs and the list underneath switches immediately; pressing a tab drops the cursor into that list, and pressing the gear opens Settings. The ALL tab shows the most recent games played or in progress from every league, grouped under dividers (`─ NFL ─`, `─ MLB ─`) with the league that played most recently first. Lists start directly under the tab row. A league tab shows that league's full scoreboard inline — upcoming games, week dividers, fourteen rows per page. Pressing the tab swaps the tab row for `< PREV · NFL 1/4 · NEXT >` with the cursor on NEXT (the likely next action); press again to page forward, up moves to PREV (press to page back), up again brings the tabs back, down from NEXT enters the rows, and up from a page's first row comes back to NEXT. Scrolling past the last row wraps to the tabs. Selecting a game opens its matchup page, and BOOT returns to the same row. There is no header, date, or banner on any page. Score pages are a dense scoreboard of 50 px rows, about thirteen games per page — away score, away logo, matchup over a compact day/status line (`SUN 9/27 FINAL`, `TODAY 10:23 3RD`, `WED 9/30 8:15 PM`), home logo, home score — with no control hints at the bottom; the game detail page shows the large logos. There is no status banner while connected; when Wi-Fi drops, score pages show an OFFLINE SINCE banner with the time of the disconnection and the age of the saved scores. The physical 800 × 480 panel buffer is rotated in the renderer; hold the board vertically. Team logos appear in matchup lists, game details, and favorites.

The firmware includes 230 monochrome logos (30 MLB, 32 NFL, 30 NBA, and 138 FBS teams), so logos remain available offline. Source artwork comes from ESPN team directories; dedicated black artwork is preferred where supplied, otherwise the color artwork is thresholded onto white. Unlisted teams use an abbreviation badge. Team marks remain the property of their respective owners. Sources are recorded in `assets/logos/manifest.json`; run `.tools/venv/bin/python tools/build_logos.py` to rebuild the bundled bitmaps from the saved directories.

## Typography

Labels, scores, and team names use the Adafruit GFX pixel font at integer sizes. Secondary lines — the day/status under each matchup, records, venues, hints — use **Inter Medium rasterized at 12 px** (about 9 px capitals, proportional), which reads far better than a pixel font at that size. The day/status line under each matchup uses Inter SemiBold at 13 px (`InterRow.h`), one step larger and heavier than the captions. The bitmap font headers (`InterSmall.h`, `InterRow.h`, and `InterRead.h` for the Bible) are generated by `tools/build_font.py` from `assets/fonts/Inter-Variable.ttf`; Inter is licensed under the SIL Open Font License 1.1 (`assets/fonts/OFL.txt`). `tools/build_font.py --samples previews/font-samples.png 11:500 12:500 12:600` renders a comparison strip for other sizes and weights.

## First setup

1. Power the board through USB-C.
2. Join the `PixelLeague-…` Wi-Fi network shown on its screen, using the displayed password.
3. Open **http://192.168.4.1** on that phone or computer.
4. Enter your **2.4 GHz** home Wi-Fi name/password and choose your timezone.
5. Once the display says connected, press BOOT twice to return to the league menu. Rejoin your normal Wi-Fi on your phone/computer.

The phone page checks the connection and displays **Connected!**, a clear error, or instructions if the phone loses the setup hotspot. Tap **Finish Setup** when connected. The setup hotspot closes after finishing, two minutes after a successful connection, or after ten minutes. Open **Wi-Fi + Settings → Connect / Change Wi-Fi** to restart it. Wi-Fi credentials are stored on the device, not in source code.

## Idle sleep and battery

After ten minutes without a button press the board shows the verse of the day (or the current page when no Bible is loaded) behind a centered two-line banner across the top of the panel — `PRESS ANY BUTTON FOR LATEST` over `ASLEEP - 1:42 PM - BATTERY 78%`, both in the 12 px pixel font — cuts panel power, and deep-sleeps. Before sleeping it also puts the audio codec into standby, cuts its analog rail and the amplifier, and holds the panel lines low, so nothing but the ESP32's RTC domain and the PMU draws from the battery. Any rocker direction, rocker press, or BOOT wakes it; it reconnects and refreshes within about five seconds. While asleep the board does not refresh scores at all — the sleep screen shows the verse of the day, so nothing on it goes stale — and its only timed wake is **6:30 am**, when it syncs the devotional library, picks the new verse, refreshes the weather, checks for a firmware update, and fetches every league so the morning launcher is current, then sleeps again. Any button wakes it and refreshes everything within a few seconds. While awake, polling is **game-aware**: a league with a game on (or starting within fifteen minutes) is polled every minute, an idle league every fifteen. `Z` over USB prints the time to the 6:30 wake and which leagues are live. The tab is remembered across sleeps in RTC memory. It never sleeps during Wi-Fi setup, a voice request, or speech. Battery percentage comes from the AXP2101 fuel gauge and also shows in Settings. USB `I` forces an idle sleep for testing.

## Nightly sleep

At 11:00 pm local time the board shows the verse of the day under an `ASLEEP - PRESS ANY BUTTON` / `BACK AT 6:30 AM - BATTERY nn%` banner, cuts panel power (the e-paper keeps the image), and deep-sleeps with a timer set for 6:30 am; a BOOT press wakes it earlier. Only the 11 pm transition triggers it, so waking it by hand at night keeps it awake until the next evening. **Settings → SLEEP 11PM-6:30AM: ON/OFF** turns the schedule off; the manual SLEEP DISPLAY entry (BOOT to wake, no timer) is unchanged. The schedule needs a valid clock and does not fire during Wi-Fi setup. USB disappears while asleep, so the `device_console` tools cannot reach a sleeping board.

## Launcher

The first screen is a launcher with three doors: the weather strip, the Bible row — today's reference on the left, `DEVOTIONAL >` on the right, the verse beneath — and the score tab strip (ALL/MLB/NFL/NBA/CFB plus the settings gear), which is selected by default — moving along the tabs switches the list beneath, and pressing a tab opens that scoreboard with the list already entered with the latest games of every league beneath it, grouped by league with dividers, as many as fit. Rocker up/down moves between the two bars and press opens the one selected: BIBLE goes to the Bible home (`CONTINUE <book chapter> page/pages`, `VERSE OF THE DAY`, `BOOKS`), SPORTS SCORES goes to the scoreboard home with its ALL/MLB/NFL/NBA/CFB tabs and the settings gear. BOOT from either home returns to the launcher. The board boots and wakes to the launcher, and a refresh wake fetches all four feeds so the launcher's scores are current.

## Daily devotional

Alongside the verse of the day the board writes a one-screen devotional for kids aged 7–13: the verse up top, a **title**, one **TRUTH** sentence, three short bullets, a **DO IT TODAY** action, and a one-line **PRAY**. Each day's devotional is a Markdown file in the repo — `devotionals/MM-DD.md` with `ref:` (optional; it becomes that day's verse of the day on the launcher) and `title:` in the front matter, then `## Truth` (one sentence and three bullets), `## Do it today` and `## Pray`. `tools/build_devotionals.py` validates every file against the one-screen limits and exports `devotionals/out/MM-DD.json`; commit and push, and the device keeps a copy of the whole library on its data partition: whenever it is online — at a wake every six hours, and in the background while awake — it fetches `devotionals/out/index.json` (day → content hash) and downloads only the days that are new or changed (up to forty per pass), so every day's devotional reads offline and a pushed edit reaches a device within six hours. `tools/upload_bible.sh` pre-loads the library with the Bible image. `device_console.py devosync` syncs now. `--draft 7` (or `all`) has the model write drafts for days that have no file yet, for you to edit. A day with no file falls back to a devotional the model writes on the device from the verse and its neighbours. Either way the result is cached in NVS so it reads offline and survives sleeps. The launcher's Bible row (`DEVOTIONAL >`) opens it, as does **Bible home → TODAY'S DEVOTIONAL**; three buttons along the bottom — **READ ALOUD** (through the speaker), **BIBLE** (the reader home: continue, verse of the day, books) and **DONE** (back to the launcher) — and BOOT is the same as DONE. The brief is gospel-centered: the truth points to what God has done in Jesus before anything we do in response. Voice: "what's today's devotional" opens it, "read today's devotional" opens and reads it. Without a voice key the page explains it needs one. `device_console.py devotional` writes a fresh one over USB; the sample used in the previews is `tests/fixtures/devotional.json`.

## Weather

A strip across the top of the launcher shows the weather: a 56 px glyph (sun, moon, sun-and-cloud, cloud, rain, snow, storm, fog), the current temperature at pixel size 3, the condition in the reading font, and on the right the battery percentage with a cell glyph filled to the level (read from the PMU fuel gauge every minute); press the strip for the 7-day forecast page, which carries the high, low, rain chance and city. Data is **Open-Meteo** (free, no key, one small HTTPS request) for the board's location, which comes from the optional **ZIP or city for weather** field on the Wi-Fi setup page (geocoded by Open-Meteo) or, if that is blank, from the public IP (`ipinfo.io/json`, which can land a county or two off), and is kept in NVS (`lat`, `lon`, `city`). `device_console.py location --text 44077` sets it over USB and refetches. The forecast refreshes hourly while awake and on each 15-minute refresh wake when it is older than 50 minutes, and the last reading is cached in NVS so the strip survives restarts and offline periods. The **forecast page** shows now (large glyph, temperature, condition, high/low/rain) and seven 78 px rows — `TODAY`, `THU 10/1`, … — each with a 56 px glyph, condition and rain chance at pixel size 2, and the high (size 3) and low, plus the time of the last update; BOOT returns to the launcher. Voice has the whole week in its context: "what's the weather", "will it rain tomorrow", "do I need a jacket this weekend" open the forecast page and, with spoken replies on, say a one-sentence answer (the `open_weather` action). The scope line is now sports, the Bible and the weather. Both hosts chain to ISRG Root X1, already in the trusted roots. `device_console.py weather` forces a refetch and prints the result.

## Bible and verse of the day

The whole **Berean Standard Bible** (BSB, dedicated to the public domain by its publishers) lives on the board: 66 books, 1,189 chapters, 3.7 MB of text packed to 1.3 MB — one zlib file per book, decompressed on the device by the ROM inflater when a book is opened — on the LittleFS data partition (10.9 MB), so it reads offline and leaves room for more translations. The Bible home offers `CONTINUE <book chapter> page/pages`, a `TODAY'S DEVOTIONAL` box with the day's verse printed inside it (the verse of the day is the devotional's opening), and `BOOKS OF THE BIBLE`, where the Old Testament fills two columns and the New Testament the third, divided by a rule. The reader lays a chapter out in **Inter Medium at a 20 px em** (capitals about 14 px, the height of the scoreboard's pixel font) on 26 px lines, 27 lines a page, with verse numbers inline; rocker down/up turns pages and rolls straight into the next or previous chapter (and book), press opens the book list (three columns) and then a chapter grid, BOOT goes back. The reading position is saved to NVS on every page turn and restored after sleep or power loss. The verse of the day is picked from a table of 300+ well-known verses by the day of the year, so it needs no network and every device agrees; it changes at midnight (or at the next refresh wake while asleep). The idle and nightly sleep screens show the verse large and centered under the banner instead of a stale scoreboard.

Voice reaches it too: "open the Bible", "go to Psalm 23", "John 3:16", "read me Romans 8" (spoken through the same TTS path, from the chapter file rather than the model), "verse of the day". A verse asked for by number lands on its page highlighted.

Loading the text is a one-time step separate from the firmware flash: `.tools/venv/bin/python tools/build_bible.py` downloads `bsb.txt` into `.tools/bible/`, normalizes six typographic characters to ASCII (the fonts cover 32–126), checks the book table and every verse-of-the-day reference in `Bible.h` against the text, and writes one zlib file per book (`/bible/bsb/43.z` is John, chapters separated by a record separator, verses one per line). `tools/upload_bible.sh <port>` packs them with `mklittlefs` and flashes the image to the data partition (offset and size from `partitions.csv`); the score caches on that partition are wiped and rebuild on the next fetch. The Bible then survives every later firmware flash. `device_console.py bible --text "John 3:16"` (or `--text daily`) opens the reader over USB.

## Dark mode

**Settings → DARK MODE: ON/OFF** inverts the whole display — black background, white text and logos — on every page including the sleep screen, and persists across restarts. On a 1-bit e-paper panel this is a full-frame inversion applied after rendering, so partial refreshes stay consistent; the panel spends slightly more time settling on a mostly black image.

## Voice

Hold the rocker, ask about a team, a score, or a league, and let go. The board records through its ES8311 codec (12 kHz mono, up to 10 s, in PSRAM) and sends the clip as WAV in one chat-completion request to OpenRouter (`google/gemini-2.5-flash`, audio input). The model classifies the request against the pages the device already has and the board **goes straight there**: "Bears score" opens the Bears' most recent matchup page, "Bears next game" opens the Bears team page, "MLB scores" opens the MLB scoreboard. A specific matchup ("Rams Broncos") opens that game. Only when no page fits — or the information isn't in the saved scores — does it show what it heard plus a short written answer (with an **OPEN …** row if a league or team was involved), and speak it. If a requested game isn't in the saved feeds it falls back to the team page, then the league page.

**Sports history and trivia:** questions about past seasons, championships, records, rules or players ("Who won the 1985 Super Bowl?", "How many World Series have the Yankees won?", "How long is a football field?") are answered from the model's own knowledge in a sentence or two, shown and spoken, with the season or year and an honest "I'm not sure" when it isn't. Current and recent scores never come from memory — those stay grounded in the device's saved feeds. The assistant is scoped to sports: anything else gets "I only know about sports. Ask me about a team, a game, or sports history!"

Anything about the present — the current starter, coach or roster, "last year", "the latest", totals that can still grow — is never answered from the model's memory (which ends before today): it says "I'm not sure about that one." and flags the question for a **lookup**: the board then makes one more request with OpenRouter's web-search plugin attached (three results), still under the sports-only instructions, shows "LOOKING THAT UP…" meanwhile, and presents the plain-text answer with the citation links stripped. A lookup adds about 3–4 s and roughly ¾ of a cent; classic trivia never triggers one. The model is given only the device's saved scores (the four league feeds, newest first, plus favorites and the current time) and is instructed to answer from nothing else, so answers are grounded in what the screen can show; if the answer isn't in the saved scores it says so. Audio is recorded only while the rocker is held and is not stored after the request.

**Spoken replies:** after the answer appears, the board reads it aloud through its speaker. The answer text goes in a second, streamed request to `openai/gpt-audio-mini` (OpenRouter only returns speech when streaming), which sends back 24 kHz PCM in base64 chunks; the board reassembles them in PSRAM and plays them through the ES8311 and the amplifier (enabled only while speaking). The speech model is framed as a read-aloud narrator so it repeats the text verbatim rather than commenting on it. **Settings → SPOKEN REPLIES: ON/OFF** mutes this; the choice persists. A spoken reply adds about 2–3 seconds and a fraction of a cent.

**Latency:** the TLS handshake to OpenRouter (about a second on this chip) starts the moment you hold the rocker, so it is finished before you let go, and the same connection carries the speech request. The clip is recorded at 12 kHz (transcribes as well as 16 kHz) and trimmed of leading/trailing silence before upload, and speech plays as it streams in rather than after the download. Typical: answer on screen about 3 s after release, speech starting about 2.5 s after that.

**Settings → VOICE: ALLOY (PRESS TO HEAR)** cycles through the eight voices gpt-audio-mini offers (alloy, ash, ballad, coral, echo, sage, shimmer, verse), speaks a sample line in each, and saves the choice; it applies to answers, weather, and the devotional. The OpenRouter key is stored on the device (NVS), never in source: enter it in the optional **Voice key** field of the Wi-Fi setup page, or send it over USB with `.tools/venv/bin/python tools/device_console.py key` (reads `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` from `~/.hermes/.env`). Without a key, or offline, holding the rocker explains why voice can't run and releasing returns to the scores. Each question costs roughly $0.0003 and takes 2–4 seconds. Diagnostics: `device_console.py record` saves 3 s from the microphone to `previews/mic.wav` with level statistics; `device_console.py ask` simulates a 5 s hold and prints the reply. Trusted roots include GTS Root R4 for openrouter.ai.

Open a league to see its recent games, newest first, whether or not anything is scheduled today: the last 7 days for MLB and NBA, 28 for the NFL, and 8 for college football. Each row shows its day (TODAY, SUN 09/27, …), and football lists are divided by week (WEEK 3, WILD CARD, SUPER BOWL, …) using ESPN's week numbers. The same scoreboard layout (page bar, week dividers, thirteen rows) also serves the standalone league page used by voice and single-day views; single-day views keep a date header so you can tell which day you are on. Opening a game shows the matchup page: large logos toward the edges with the day, the "@", and the venue in the column between them, each team's record beneath its logo, abbreviations and scores centered under them, the status in the gap between the scores, and then game details fetched on demand — score by period (quarters, or innings with runs/hits/errors), a stat table with a column per team, and ESPN's recap headline for the game. Football details come from ESPN's game summary and list each team's passing, rushing, and receiving leader (surname and yards), total yards, and turnovers; basketball shows FG%, 3PT%, rebounds, assists, and each team's points and rebounds leader; baseball shows hits and errors in the line score and each team's home-run, RBI, and batting leader. ESPN only publishes line scores and stats for baseball games on the day they are played, so older MLB matchups show records and the headline only. Live games refresh their details every minute.

On the matchup page, up/down cycles the selection through the away logo and the home logo (a thick frame marks the choice); press to open that team's page: its results newest first plus its next scheduled game, with a top row that adds or removes the team from Favorites. BOOT returns to the matchup. Favorites live under Settings → FAVORITE TEAMS and open the same team pages. Sleep and spoken-reply settings are there too; BOOT wakes the display from sleep.

**Standings:** the bottom of a team page has its division and conference standings (`NFC NORTH STANDINGS`, `NFC STANDINGS`; MLB and NBA the same way; college teams get their conference, e.g. SEC). The standings page is a ranked table — logo, team, W-L, PCT (NFL) or games behind (MLB/NBA) or conference record (college), streak — with the team you came from highlighted; up/down flips between the division and the conference, BOOT returns to the team page. Voice gets there too: "NFC North standings", "AL East standings", "SEC standings", or "where are the Bears in the standings". Pro standings come from ESPN's division-level standings feed (one request per league, cached six hours); college standings are fetched per conference (the full college tree is 2.6 MB, too large to parse on the board), using the conference id ESPN attaches to each college team.

Team pages come from ESPN's team schedule feed (full-season results for the NFL and college football) merged with the team's games from the league feed, which covers MLB and NBA where the schedule feed only lists the current segment. Each team page is cached (up to 24 files).

## Scores and offline behavior

- Scores come directly from ESPN's HTTPS scoreboard feeds; no personal API key or running Mac is required. Trusted roots: ISRG Root X1/X2 (ESPN) and GTS Root R4 (OpenRouter, for voice).
- College football is **FBS**, not every college division.
- This is an unofficial integration with a publicly reachable feed, without a documented availability guarantee. It is intended as a personal prototype; review provider terms before distribution/commercial use.
- ESPN's scoreboard rejects date ranges, so the recent-games feed is assembled on the device one day at a time: today is refreshed every cycle, yesterday while any of its games are still going, and uncovered days are backfilled a few per cycle (about 1.5 seconds apart) until the window is full. NFL and college responses already cover the rest of their week, so most backfill requests are a few kilobytes.
- The active view updates every 60 seconds after success; failures retry after 120 seconds. The home screen cycles the four league feeds, one request cycle per minute, so opening a league is instant. Network reconnect retries back off to 60 seconds.
- Each feed and each league/date has a separate last-successful-update timestamp. In-progress games older than three minutes or affected by connectivity/service errors are labeled saved.
- Cached feeds (one per league), day snapshots, and favorites persist across restarts. Up to 56 league/date snapshots are retained, pruning the oldest dates first.
- A Wi-Fi outage shows **OFFLINE SINCE <time>** with **SCORES FROM <last update>** on score pages. An API failure while connected shows no banner; cached in-progress status is labeled **SAVED** and is never advanced locally.
- If there is no saved data for a view, the screen says so. Empty schedules and missing scores are distinct.
- Time is initialized from the phone during setup when available, synchronized from NTP, and written to the onboard PCF85063 in UTC. The app trusts the RTC only after it has set it successfully. Timezone display handles daylight saving time for the supplied US zones. If the RTC loses power and internet is unavailable, saved timestamps remain available but today's date cannot be established until synchronization.

## Build

The development tools are project-local in `.tools/` and excluded from version control. Run `tools/bootstrap.sh` to reproduce them; it requires internet access, macOS Apple Silicon, Python 3, Node.js, and the Xcode command-line tools.

```sh
./tools/bootstrap.sh
./tools/test.sh
./tools/build.sh
```

Pinned dependencies: Arduino ESP32 **3.3.0**, ArduinoJson **7.4.2**, Adafruit GFX **1.12.1**, Adafruit BusIO **1.17.4**, esptool **4.9.0** for local USB diagnostics/backups. Arduino CLI version is recorded in `.tools/arduino-cli-version.txt`.

The panel is driven over hardware SPI at 20 MHz (as in Waveshare's ESP-IDF port) and refreshes in the background: a key press updates the screen state immediately and is drawn the moment the panel is free, so rapid presses never stall the device. A partial update takes about 0.5 s of panel time. Ghost-cleaning full refreshes happen only when the device is idle — a 1.7 s fast refresh after eight partial updates and a slow full refresh every 30 minutes.

The board is configured for 16 MB flash, 8 MB OPI PSRAM, native USB Serial/JTAG, and a custom partition table with two 4 MB application slots and a persistent LittleFS partition. Firmware sources are in `firmware/RetroSports/`. `tools/patch_esp32.py` disables an unsafe raw DNS-cache reset in the framework for this single-interface application; normal DNS TTL expiry remains active. Clock synchronization uses the thread-safe ESP-IDF SNTP service.

`tools/flash.sh /dev/cu.usbmodem1101` installs the compiled firmware. Flashing replaces the factory application. The original firmware backup, when successfully captured, lives in `backups/original-firmware.bin`; backups are private and excluded from version control. Do not publish them because they can contain device settings.

## Updates over Wi-Fi

Devices update themselves from the project's GitHub releases, so a board living somewhere else still gets new builds. `tools/release.sh 1.5.0 "notes"` sets `FIRMWARE_VERSION` in `Version.h`, runs the tests and build, commits, tags `v1.5.0`, pushes, and creates a release carrying `RetroSports.bin` and `version.json`. Each device looks at `releases/latest/download/version.json` at its 6:30 am wake (silently, as part of that wake's fetches) and on demand from **Settings → CHECK FOR UPDATES (v…)**; a newer version is downloaded into the inactive app slot (~1.7 MB, about half a minute), the board shows `INSTALLED V… - RESTARTING` and reboots into it. The new build marks itself good once Wi-Fi and the clock are up (or after two minutes); until then the board will not sleep, and if it never gets that far the bootloader rolls back to the previous build on the next boot. The Bible files and all settings survive updates. `device_console.py update` runs a check over USB. The trusted roots include USERTrust ECC (github.com) and ISRG Root X1 (the release asset host).

## Diagnostics and verification

```sh
.tools/venv/bin/python tools/device_console.py '?'
.tools/venv/bin/python tools/device_console.py capture
```

USB diagnostics support `u`, `d`, `s`, `b` for navigation, `h`/`r` for hold/release, and `?` for status (including the display pipeline: partial-update count, pending/busy state, idle time, and queued keys). Every draw logs `DRAW page= send=` when the frame is sent and `DRAW done ms=` when the panel finishes; idle cleanups log `CLEAN fast` or `CLEAN full`. They do not log Wi-Fi passwords. Screen capture exports the real framebuffer to `previews/device.png`; a setup-screen capture includes the temporary hotspot password, so keep it private.

`tools/test.sh` checks button debounce and hold exclusivity, date/DST boundaries, actual provider fixtures, cache round trips, malformed-response retention, freshness, missing-score handling, portal connection states, and bounded response-buffer allocation. It also renders previews using the **same renderer and Adafruit GFX code** as the firmware. Files labeled `demo` contain synthetic data only for visual review; they are never compiled into the device program.

See `VALIDATION.md` for checks actually completed on this device and remaining hands-on checks.
